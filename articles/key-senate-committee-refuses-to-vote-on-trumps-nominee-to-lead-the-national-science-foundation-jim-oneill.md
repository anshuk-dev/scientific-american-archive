# Key Senate committee refuses to vote on Trump’s nominee to lead the National Science Foundation, Jim O’Neill

*The decision came hours after the White House pulled the nomination of Heidi Overton to lead the Food and Drug Administration*

Just hours after the Trump administration dropped its nominee to lead the Food and Drug Administration, a key Senate committee pulled the plug on a vote for the White House nominee to lead the National Science Foundation (NSF) as well.

On Tuesday the White House announced it would not move forward with Heidi Overton’s candidacy for commissioner of the Food and Drug Administration. And on Wednesday, during opening remarks for a hearing of the Senate Committee on Health, Education, Labor and Pensions, Senator Bill Cassidy of Louisiana said Jim O’Neill, who previously served as acting director of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, would not be considered to lead the NSF.

O’Neill “did not do enough to stand up for science and against irresponsible decisions and statements from [Secretary of Health and Human Services Robert F. Kennedy, Jr.],” Cassidy said. “His role in the firing of Dr. Susan Monarez raised further concerns about the independent judgment he would bring to NSF.”

Monarez was fired as CDC director in 2025, a move she blamed on her refusal to approve vaccine recommendations that lacked evidence or to fire agency employees without cause. Kennedy, whose department oversees the CDC, is a longtime vaccine skeptic. O’Neill is widely considered a key Kennedy ally and a proponent of the “Make America Healthy Again” movement. Many science advocates and organizations such as a the Union of Concerned Scientists had campaigned against his nomination, citing his actions during his time as acting CDC director and his background in biotechnology investment as reasons he should not be considered for the job.

The White House did not immediately respond to a request for comment, while a spokesperson for the NSF declined to comment.

In a post on X in the wake of Overton’s removal, Cassidy said she had a “viable confirmation path,” but the White House did not believe she had the votes.

“This was odd as I was having productive conversations with members,” Cassidy wrote. “I believe she would have had the votes to pass out of committee and pass the full Senate. My conversations with her convinced me she would follow the science and push back against decisions that put Americans’ health at risk.”

Cassidy alleged that the only issue that stood in the way of Overton’s confirmation was that antiabortion senators had sought assurances from the White House that the FDA would back their agenda.

“The Administration withdrew Dr. Overton rather than make such commitments,” he wrote. “Unfortunately, with the limited time left, confirming a permanent FDA Commissioner will fall to Senate Democrats.”

Although Overton had praised Trump during her confirmation hearing in September, she also distanced herself from some of his more outlandish positions, telling the committee that Tylenol use during pregnancy has not been found to cause autism and that the mumps, measles and rubella (MMR) vaccine is safe and effective. She also said the abortion drug mifepristone met FDA safety and efficacy standards. The drug has been at the center of legal efforts to reduce abortion access, with antiabortion proponents falsely claiming the drug is unsafe.
