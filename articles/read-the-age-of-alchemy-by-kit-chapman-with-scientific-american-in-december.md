Book Club: Read The Age of Alchemy, by Kit Chapman, with Scientific American

The history of chemistry predates science itself; for thousands of years, kings and servants relied on alchemists to concoct “magical” ointments and elixirs. That practice morphed over the years into a formal field of investigation. Chemistry is the field of science that tells us what stars are made of, what’s safe to eat and how our species will live on a changing planet in the future. Chemistry is everywhere and connects to nearly everything. As Kit Chapman says in the prologue of The Age of Alchemy: How Early Innovators Shaped Modern Chemistry: “Before the Enlightenment, there were only wizards, witches and wishful thinking,” but slowly a “new science arrived to change our world.” The scientific study of chemistry has saved lives, caused massive destruction and informed a greater understanding of who we are and what we’re made of.

This month Scientific American staff will be reading The Age of Alchemy together, and on December 16 we’ll share our staff discussion in our Science Quickly podcast. We’ll talk about our favorite chemistry facts and the places where we encounter chemistry in our everyday lives.

We hope you join us in reading this exciting book! Stay tuned all month for more insights on The Age of Alchemy, including in our pop culture newsletter Cultured. I’ll be highlighting segments that stick out to us and answering your reader questions in our weekly Books Corner segment.

Join our discussion where we've posted some questions to get us started.
